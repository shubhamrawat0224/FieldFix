# Amperia Customer Escalation and Communication Rules

## Standard Escalation Triggers
An escalation is required whenever the issue crosses operational, safety, or billing boundaries. Examples include locked access with no approved customer contact, an out-of-contract repair, a unit that has failed due to a missing part or unsafe environment, or a customer refusal to sign required documentation after work is completed.

## Customer Communication Sequence
When the technician identifies a condition that affects safety or liability, the technician must first explain the issue to the customer in plain language, document the explanation, and then route the service note to dispatch. The dispatch team is responsible for the next operational decision regarding warranty, rescheduling, or supervisor review.

## Missing Signatures
A customer signature is considered part of the final verification for completed work, especially when equipment is repaired or replaced. If a signature is unavailable for a controlled repair, the technician must note the reason, document the failed attempt, and escalate to dispatch for corrective instruction.

## Warranty and Contract Exceptions
Not all products are covered by Amperia service plans. The technician must check whether the asset is still under warranty, the maintenance window is still active, or the failure is caused by customer misuse, weather exposure, or unauthorized repair history. The final note must state which provider, contract, or internal policy is responsible.

## Service Recommendations
The customer-facing recommendation should explain the issue in terms of operational risk, expected repair path, and approximate next action. A clear recommendation reduces re-dispatch and supports better legal and billing documentation.
