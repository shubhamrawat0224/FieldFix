# Amperia Company Policy

## Purpose
Amperia serves commercial and residential customers by providing fast, safe, and compliant field service for refrigeration, HVAC, and general equipment support. The company policy requires every technician to document field findings before recommending a replacement, escalation, or customer charge.

## Service Standards
Technicians are expected to evaluate the equipment in order, confirm the asset model and serial number, record the customer's location and access constraints, and check whether the service call falls within the active maintenance window or warranty schedule. Any condition that introduces a safety risk, such as exposed wiring, gas odor, or inoperable lockout/tagout controls, must be reported immediately to dispatch and the customer contact.

## Decision Authority
Field technicians are not authorized to override dispatch or service approval for out-of-scope work without documented supervisor approval. If the failure requires a new part, additional labor, or a nonstandard procedure, the technician must update the work order with the reason, an estimate, and the required next approval step.

## Customer Communication
Every service visit must include a brief summary of the issue, the work performed, and the customer-facing recommendation. If the customer refuses work or access, the note must include the refusal details, site conditions, and any follow-up obligations.

## Documentation
The company requires a clear timeline: symptom observed, diagnostic steps, measurements collected, parts inspected, and final recommendation. Notes should be factual, specific, and free of speculation. Only approved service codes should be used in the system.

## Safety Culture
Amperia prioritizes safe work over speed. If a technician cannot safely complete the task, the job must be paused and escalated through the approved route. The service team may resume work only after the safety concern is addressed, a supervisor approves the plan, and the customer is informed of the risk.

## Role of Knowledge Base
Private operational knowledge, such as equipment troubleshooting, escalation criteria, and part handling rules, supplements but does not replace standard procedures. When a situation touches multiple policy areas, technicians must apply the most restrictive rule and document the combination of factors involved.
