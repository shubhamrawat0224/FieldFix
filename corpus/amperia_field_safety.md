# Amperia Field Safety and Access Rules

## Site Safety First
Technicians must complete a site walk before beginning work. This includes checking trip hazards, live electrical exposure, wet floors, unstable ladders, customer traffic, and any environmental alert affecting access. If the site is unsafe, the technician must stop work and contact dispatch before performing diagnosis or repair.

## Hazard Response
A technician must stop work and escalate immediately for any of the following: active gas odor, leaking refrigerant, smoke, arcing electrical components, damaged breaker panels, or nonfunctional lockout/tagout equipment. The technician may not continue by improvising a workaround when the equipment condition could injure a person or create property damage.

## Access and Customer Presence
If the customer is not present or the property is secured, technicians must verify the access method, site entry rules, and whether the owner has granted permission for service. A locked gate, missing access key, or blocked service panel is not an automatic reason to proceed without a site supervisor or customer contact approval.

## Weather and Site Constraints
During weather alerts or severe site conditions, the work plan must be re-evaluated. External temperature extremes, storms, poor ventilation, and unsafe roof access can change the safe operation of equipment and require suspension or rescheduling.

## Personal Protective Equipment
Technicians must use the required PPE for the task: insulated gloves for electrical work, eye protection for debris, hearing protection around loud equipment, and slip-resistant footwear for wet or greasy access areas. When a task exceeds the approved safe scope, the technician must request additional support or a supervisor review.

## Documentation of Safety Concerns
The safety concern, customer notification, dispatch contact, and approval or refusal must be recorded in the work order. Technical notes must distinguish between a customer refusal, a site hazard, and a true equipment defect so the follow-up team understands the risk condition.
