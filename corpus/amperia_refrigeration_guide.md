# Amperia Refrigeration Equipment Guide

## Core System Components
Refrigeration systems rely on a compressor, condenser coil, fan motor, evaporator coil, expansion device, and refrigerant circuit. The relationship between these components matters: if the airflow is restricted or the condenser is overheated, the compressor may fail under load even when the customer reports a single symptom.

## Common Failure Signals
A refrigeration case or walk-in unit that is not cooling may show one or more of the following: high head pressure, low evaporator airflow, warm product temperatures, repeated short cycling, or a fan motor that fails to start. Frost buildup on the evaporator can indicate restricted airflow, failed defrost control, or a refrigerant-related issue. A compressor that starts and then trips on overload is often a sign of overheating, failed capacitor, or a blocked condenser.

## Diagnostic Discipline
The technician should verify the asset nameplate, confirm the setpoint and compressor amp draw, inspect the condenser and evaporator coils, and test the fan motors before replacing major components. A visual check of the compressor condition, line temperatures, and defrost cycle can distinguish a simple control issue from an underlying compressor failure.

## Service Recommendation Rules
If the compressor is failed and the unit is under a service window with valid access and customer approval, the technician may recommend repair within policy. If the customer will not provide a signature for the final work, or if the site access is restricted, the technician must escalate the paperwork and approval path before any further action.

## Preventive Considerations
Long-term reliability depends on clean coils, tight electrical connections, proper fan operation, and compliance with the refrigeration cycle. Technicians should avoid replacing compressor parts without confirming the underlying cause, because repeated compressor failures often result from poor airflow or high head pressure rather than a single part defect.
